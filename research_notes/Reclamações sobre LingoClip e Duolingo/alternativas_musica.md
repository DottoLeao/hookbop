# Competitive landscape: "learn languages with music" products (other than LingoClip and Duolingo)

Research date: 2026-10-21. All dates are as shown on the cited pages. Review text is paraphrased (not quoted verbatim) and attributed to reviewer handle and date. App Store "latest version" dates often omit the year; the year is inferred from version order and flagged where that happens.

**Critical framing fact:** LyricsTraining is not an independent alternative to LingoClip. The iOS app that used to be "LyricsTraining" (App Store id1192698323) is now listed as **LingoClip by Elasthink S.L.**, and its version history says version 2.2.2 (03/11/2022) renamed the app from LyricsTraining to LingoClip — [App Store BR listing](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/lyricstraining/id1192698323). lingoclip.com carries a "Looking for lyricstraining.com?" banner linking to the old site — [lingoclip.com](https://lingoclip.com/). So lyricstraining.com is effectively the legacy web front end of the same product family. Everything below about LyricsTraining should be read as "LingoClip, web edition".

---

## 1. Which products exist, and how each works (mechanic, platform, song source, lyrics source/sync, business model, size, ratings)

### Takeaway
The category splits into (a) free/freemium YouTube-based gap-fill games (LyricsTraining/LingoClip, Sounter, Fill the Song, LyricsGaps, Lyrkit), (b) paid, licensed, lesson-structured song apps (Lirica, LyricFluent, Lingopie Music), (c) lyrics platforms with translation add-ons (Musixmatch, Letras.mus.br/Letras Academy, Genius), and (d) general video-immersion tools where music videos are one content type (FluentU, Language Reactor, Lingopie). In Brazil the visible players are LingoClip, Sounter, Letras.mus.br (Studio Sol) and a new Brazilian-linked entrant, Fill the Song, which is pitched almost exactly like the planned PWA (free gap-fill, ranks, leaderboard, PT/ES interface).

### Cited Findings

**LyricsTraining (lyricstraining.com), the web version of LingoClip, Elasthink S.L.**
- Mechanic: fill the gaps in the lyrics while the video plays, plus a karaoke mode — [lyricstraining.com](https://lyricstraining.com/). Modes are Karaoke (captioned YouTube video with highlighted lyrics), Choice-Mode (multiple choice) and Write-Mode (typing). Levels run Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced and Expert, with Write-Mode blanking 10% of words at Beginner and 100% at Expert. There is a worldwide ranking and a "Friend Challenge" — [FLTMAG, M. Fiori, 2021-10-19](https://fltmag.com/lyricstraining/).
- Song catalogue in 13 languages: English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, Italian, German, Dutch, Japanese (Romaji), Turkish, Polish, Swedish, Finnish, Catalan. The interface comes in EN/ES/FR. Songs are credited to usernames and show play counts (e.g., "Lemon Tree", ~986,855 plays). Thumbnails are served from YouTube. The footer reads "© 2025 LyricsTraining.com" and says the site is for personal and educational use only. The site gives no total user or song counts — [lyricstraining.com](https://lyricstraining.com/).
- Song source: YouTube videos. Educators can add songs from YouTube through "My Lyrics", and LyricsTraining reviews them before publishing. That makes the catalogue partly user-contributed, with lyrics typed or edited by contributors — [FLTMAG 2021](https://fltmag.com/lyricstraining/).
- Business model: free on the web with "some ads", while some app features need premium — [FLTMAG 2021](https://fltmag.com/lyricstraining/). The free app version allows 3 plays per day. Premium costs R$16,90/month or R$99,90/year and adds unlimited songs, translations, full vocabulary and activity history — [Canaltech, 2021-09-10](https://canaltech.com.br/apps/como-aprender-ingles-com-musica-com-app-lyrics-training/). The current BR App Store listing shows R$16,90 or R$19,90 monthly and R$99,90 or R$119,90 annual — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/lyricstraining/id1192698323).
- Ratings and activity (the app, now LingoClip): 4.8/5 from ~1.9k ratings in the BR App Store. Recent versions are 2.6.0 (20/11/2025), 2.6.1 (12/12/2025), 2.7.0 (27 Feb), 2.8.0 (18 Apr) and 2.8.1 (28 Apr); the last three are presumably 2026 since they come after Dec 2025. Version 2.4.4 (11/09/2024) added playlist goals, levels and sharing. Version 2.3.2 (09/02/2024) added user-created, shareable playlists — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/lyricstraining/id1192698323). Older regional AppFollow snapshots of the same app ID showed 4.0 from very few ratings (7 and 32) — [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/lyricstraining/1192698323?country=ch).
- Third-party reviewers describe it as free, with a limited free version that needs no account — [ECML inventory](https://www.ecml.at/fr/Resources/ICT-tools/InventoryID/184).

**Sounter ("Sounter - Aprender Inglês" / "Aprender Inglês com Música"), Sounter Inc / seller David Galarza**
- Mechanic: music videos with synced lyrics and fill-in-the-blank exercises whose difficulty adapts to performance. Also offers karaoke with real-time pronunciation scoring, an audio-only transcription mode, offline downloads of lyrics and videos, hundreds of themed playlists, a community for sharing playlists and achievements, a teacher platform, stats and daily reminders. No leaderboard is mentioned. Languages: English (US), Italian, French; release notes for 2.7.0 also mention Russian and Korean. The listing claims ~5 million users but gives no song count and does not name a song source — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/sounter-aprender-ingles/id1505600864).
- Business model: freemium with a large price ladder in BR. Monthly R$14,90 or R$59,90; six-month R$61,90 or R$199,90; annual R$99,90 to R$299,90; welcome offer R$99,90; lifetime R$599,90 — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/sounter-aprender-ingles/id1505600864).
- Ratings and activity: 4.9/5 from ~2.7k ratings in BR. Version 2.8.4 is dated 02/12/2025 and 2.8.6 "June 18" (presumably 2026), so the app is actively maintained — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/sounter-aprender-ingles/id1505600864). Canaltech's Brazilian roundup listed it first (Android; English and Spanish at several levels) — [Canaltech, 2020, updated 2022-06-29](https://canaltech.com.br/apps/melhores-aplicativos-para-aprender-ingles-com-musica/).

**Fill the Song (fillthesong.com), Brazilian-linked, © 2026**
- Mechanic: songs play with lyrics containing blanks that learners fill in real time. Gamification includes points, levels, ranks, streaks and a global leaderboard. The page describes a "built-in progression system", and its teacher materials are CEFR-aligned. It claims 5,000+ songs. The interface is in PT/ES/EN, and Portuguese speakers are described as the largest market. Sister sites are "Notícias em Inglês", "Lingualize" and "Ensine Inglês com Música", and contact is via a WhatsApp line — [fillthesong.com](https://fillthesong.com/en/).
- Business model: the free plan includes unlimited songs per day, the leaderboard and auto-saved progress. Premium is monthly or annual, plus a Schools program with a teacher dashboard. The page mentions no ads. Song source is not stated. The site says it is *seeking music-licensing partners*, i.e., it has no announced lyrics licence — [fillthesong.com](https://fillthesong.com/en/).
- No store ratings or independent reviews found. A web search for the product name returned nothing — [search result set, see LingoClip/Lewis School result](https://lewis-school.co.uk/uncategorized/lingoclip-learn-english-through-songs/).

**Lirica (Spanish/English/German through licensed pop songs)**
- Mechanic: each lesson pairs a pop song with a grammar or conversation concept plus vocabulary. Levels are Beginner 1, Beginner 2 and Intermediate. Activities: identify words in a song excerpt, match words to meanings, put lyrics in order, watch the music video with lyrics, and play comprehension games. Each lesson downloads separately. Artists named: Shakira, Marc Anthony, Enrique Iglesias (Spanish); Pink, Whitney Houston, Alicia Keys (English). The app was last updated 2025-09-30 (release listed as 2020-05-18). Platforms are iOS and Android — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/lirica-learn-languages).
- Business model: subscription with a free trial. Pricing was $8.49/month, $19.99/quarter or $24.99/year, with a 7-day trial. The page says Lirica has "licensing agreements with each label" and that the library grows weekly — [All Language Resources, 2024-05-20](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/lirica/). Lifetime and multi-year plans were sold via deal sites — [Macworld shop](https://shop.macworld.com/sales/lirica-premium-3-year-subscription).
- Funding and licensing: raised a $1M seed in 2019 with Sony Music among the backers. The founder said the Sony relationship helps with licensing popular songs — [EdSurge, 2019-09-23](https://edsurge.com/news/2019-09-23-lirica-raises-1-million-to-improve-app-for-language-learning-through-song).
- Ratings: reseller and marketing pages cite 4.8/5 on the App Store and a 2020 "App of the Day". These are unverified and possibly stale — [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/?p=719863). A 2024 academic review in *Technology in Language Teaching & Learning* calls it engaging, supports five languages and discusses its limitations — [DOAJ, Jun 2024](https://doaj.org/article/353a2cff75eb415385403f8e3829bc0f).

**LyricFluent**
- Mechanic: line-by-line mode, tap-to-translate with pronunciation, and spaced repetition of saved words. Free use is a daily "Song of the Day" plus a 7-day trial. Languages: Spanish (most popular), French, German, Portuguese, Italian, Russian, Dutch, Romanian, English. Platforms: iOS, Android, web — [LyricFluent FAQ](https://lyricfluent.com/faq).
- Business model: subscription, roughly $4.99–9.99/month. The company says the fee pays for licensing lyrics, royalties to artists and professional translators — [LyricFluent FAQ](https://lyricfluent.com/faq); [lyricfluent.com](https://www.lyricfluent.com).
- Catalogue size claims conflict: a third-party listing says 15,000+ songs. The company blog says 7,752 Spanish songs and 31,858 in total, and an older company post said 281 — [MWM listing](https://mwm.ai/patients/apps/lyricfluent-learn-languages/1626804408); [LyricFluent blog](https://lyricfluent.com/blog/how_to_learn_languages_with_music).

**Lingopie (and Lingopie Music)**
- Lingopie is a subscription streaming service for TV shows, podcasts and books in 15 languages, including English. It offers clickable subtitles, flashcards and quizzes, and runs on iOS, Android, Fire TV, Roku and Google TV — [lingopie.com/music](https://lingopie.com/music/). Lingopie Music is described as learning through songs in genres such as reggaeton, K-pop and Brazilian funk, with interactive lyrics: tap a word for its translation and grammar context. Launch languages were Spanish, French, German, Portuguese and Korean; the help center later adds Italian, Japanese and Russian. "Song of the Day" is iOS-only — [Lingopie blog via search](https://lingopie.com/blog/lingopie-music/); [Lingopie help center via search](https://help.lingopie.com/support/solutions/articles/150000174394-how-to-learn-languages-with-music-on-lingopie). (The direct help-center fetch returned 404 on 2026-10-21, so the help-center details come from the search snippet only.)
- Trustpilot shows 4.5/5 from ~3.5–4k reviews, and the company replied to 98% of negative reviews — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lingopie.com?page=6).

**Musixmatch (lyrics platform with a translation feature)**
- BR App Store: "Musixmatch Dynamic Lyrics", seller musiXmatch srl, 4.4/5 from ~3k ratings. Version 8.3.4 is dated "14 Jul" (year not shown). It offers translations in 60+ languages while lyrics play, and the Editors' Choice text pitches Portuguese translation as useful for language learners. Premium adds *unlimited* translations, so free translations appear to be capped. Premium is listed at $35.99/year in the US; BR in-app items range from R$6,90 to R$82,90 — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/musixmatch-letras-de-m%C3%BAsicas/id448278467).
- Translations shown in Spotify come from Musixmatch and are described as made by bilingual people, but they are not available for all songs or users — [Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Live-Ideas/Translate-Lyrics-to-other-languages/idc-p/5775878/highlight/true). The Google Play copy says translations display in time with the music — [Mixrank mirror of Play listing](https://mixrank.com/playstore/apps/com.musixmatch.android.lyrify).
- Musixmatch has no gap-fill or game mechanic. It is a lyrics, sync and translation layer.

**Letras.mus.br / Letras Academy (Studio Sol, Brazil)**
- BR App Store: 4.9/5 from ~30k ratings; seller Studio Sol Comunicação Digital LTDA; version 2.8.58 dated "7 Sep" (year not shown). Features include human-made, verse-by-verse contextual translations, "Significados" (song meanings), and real-time lyrics while listening on Spotify, Deezer and other apps. The app contains ads and its privacy label lists third-party ad targeting; Premium is ad-free. Prices: Letras Premium R$6,90/month, R$29,90/year or R$179,90 one-time. **Letras Academy** (courses and short lessons in English, Spanish and Korean that use music) costs R$29,90/month or R$239,90/year — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/letras-mus-br/id773347891).
- Another App Store listing describes Letras Academy as offering language courses and private teachers under an "Academy" tab, plus a Pronunciation Dictionary with examples taken from songs — [App Store (gw) listing via search](https://apps.apple.com/gw/app/letras-mus-br/id773347891?l=fr-FR).

**FluentU (video immersion; music videos are one content type)**
- Turns movie trailers, music videos and news clips into interactive lessons with subtitles, vocab lists and quizzes. 2026 pricing is reported inconsistently: $29.99/month anchor vs about $15–20/month monthly or $8–10/month annual — [learnenglish.life 2026 review](https://learnenglish.life/reviews/fluentu/); [TalkDrill comparison 2026 (competitor)](https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/compare/talkdrill-vs-fluentu/). FluentU embeds YouTube content on its pages and layers its transcripts on top — [Test Prep Insight](https://testprepinsight.com/reviews/fluentu-review/). Trustpilot showed 5/5 from 119 reviews (search snippet) — [Trustpilot UK](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/fluentu.com).

**Language Reactor (browser extension for Netflix/YouTube)**
- Free tier: dual subtitles, click-to-translate and basic word saving. Pro costs about $5–5.95/month (3-month $13.95, annual $39.95, per one source) and adds Anki export, speech recognition and machine translation with a quota. Desktop Chrome only; no mobile or TV — [Filmora review](https://filmora.wondershare.com/more-tips/language-reactor-review.html); [Stork](https://www.stork.ai/en/language-reactor); [FluentU review (competitor)](https://www.fluentu.com/blog/reviews/language-reactor/). The Chrome Web Store rating was reported as 4.3; the rating-count figure in the snippet ("53.4K") is garbled and unverified — [Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/language-reactor/detail/language-reactor/hoombieeljmmljlkjmnheibnpciblicm). It works on any YouTube music video but has no game layer.

**Smaller or long-tail products**
- **LyricsGaps.com**: user-submitted, moderated gap-fill exercises with Easy/Medium/Hard tags in 21 languages, plus a "Challenge" section. The footer reads "© 2016", so the site looks stale. It states no business model or licence — [lyricsgaps.com](https://www.lyricsgaps.com/).
- **Lyrkit – Inglês por músicas** (Studiya Nirvana PP, iOS BR): free; claims "1,000,000 songs with videos and lyrics" with no stated source or licence; not enough ratings to show a score. Last version is 1.3.5 (17/03/2024), so the app is **stale**, with no update in ~19 months — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/id6463414094).
- **Singit** ("Aprende inglés con Singit"): songs plus interactive lessons and tap-to-translate; 4.4 from 132 ratings in the MX store (search snippet only) — [AppFollow MX](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/aprende-ingles-con-singit/1550593137?country=mx).
- **Musíglota** (Chile, 2015): shows lyrics and translations, using Spotify or local MP3s as the library. Likely stale; no Portuguese version confirmed — [24horas.cl](https://www.24horas.cl/tendencias/tecnologia/aprender-ingles-escuchando-musica-musiglota-la-nueva-app-de-idiomas-1779410); [Xataka CO](https://www.xataka.com.co/aplicaciones/musiglota-la-app-para-aprender-ingles-cantando-llega-a-colombia).
- **Lyrically** (Product Hunt): free users get a monthly translation allowance; subscribers get unlimited — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/lyrically-learn-through-music). **Singdu** (Product Hunt): songs as interactive lessons with synced lyrics and tappable words, 7 languages — [hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/singdu).
- Canaltech's BR list also includes **Genius** (synced lyrics plus annotations explaining meaning) and **Cake** (speaking practice with songs and TV clips) — [Canaltech](https://canaltech.com.br/apps/melhores-aplicativos-para-aprender-ingles-com-musica/).

**Mango Languages**
- No music or song feature was found. Its entertainment content is "fully interactive feature films" — [consortium.lu](https://www.consortium.lu/?p=8894); [TRL](https://trl.org/resource/mango-languages/).

### Inferences
- The free, YouTube-based, gamified gap-fill niche in Brazil is now contested by at least LingoClip/LyricsTraining (strong BR ratings, paywall at 3 plays/day) and Fill the Song (free unlimited songs, leaderboard, PT interface, 2026). "Ranks + leaderboard + friend challenge" is therefore **not** a differentiator by itself: LyricsTraining has had a worldwide ranking and Friend Challenge since at least 2021, and Fill the Song advertises ranks, streaks and a leaderboard.
- Licensed, lesson-structured apps (Lirica, LyricFluent) charge US$5–9/month and justify it with licensing. Their catalogues are small or inconsistent, which suggests licensing costs cap catalogue size.
- Brazilian users already pay for music-lyrics products at low price points (Letras Premium R$6,90/month), and Studio Sol has a paid music-based language course (Letras Academy R$29,90/month). Studio Sol is a potential competitor or partner with huge reach (30k BR App Store ratings).
- Sounter's large BR price ladder (R$14,90 to R$599,90 lifetime) and 5M-user claim suggest aggressive paywall and upsell monetization. This is unverified as a user complaint; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- Google Play ratings, download counts and reviews for LingoClip/LyricsTraining, Sounter and others could not be retrieved (Play Store pages did not render through the fetch tool). BR Google Play data is missing.
- Lirica's current App Store rating and live price could not be verified. The App Store URL tried (a guessed ID) returned 404, which is **not** evidence of removal.
- Fill the Song: no launch date, song source, user count or user reviews found.
- Lingopie Music: whether English songs are available for Portuguese speakers, and music-specific pricing, could not be confirmed.
- Cifra Club: no language-learning feature was found. Whether it shares Letras Academy (same company) was not verified.
- Mango Languages: no evidence of any music feature. If the brief assumed one exists, that could not be confirmed.

## 2. What users praise and complain about

### Takeaway
Praise centres on fun, listening practice and karaoke-style replay. Complaints repeat across products: tight free limits or early paywalls, missing songs or small catalogues outside top global artists, intrusive ads, lyrics or captions out of sync, no translations or explanations in the free gap-fill, random rather than pedagogically targeted blanks, repetitive activities, mature content, billing or cancellation traps (Lingopie), high price and no speaking practice (FluentU), and platform limits (Language Reactor desktop-only).

### Cited Findings

**LyricsTraining / LingoClip**
- Praise: fun for teaching languages through songs — [UNL Tech EDGE](https://mediahub.unl.edu/media/20700). Easy to navigate, and you can replay lines until you catch the word — [British Council LearnEnglish Teens comment](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/comment/83845). A BR reviewer (Lucianoon, "27 Sep", year not shown) said even the free tier is worth downloading, at 3 songs/day — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/lyricstraining/id1192698323).
- Complaint, free limit: reviewer renatofuga (29/04/2020) liked it but asked for 5 plays/day instead of 3 before giving 5 stars — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/lyricstraining/id1192698323).
- Complaint, missing songs: reviewer Frauandrade (02/12/2020) wanted to go premium but the song they loved most (French) wasn't there on day one. The developer replied (18/12/2020) that it had been added and invited content requests — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/lyricstraining/id1192698323). A British Council learner also noted some songs aren't available — [British Council](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/comment/83845).
- Complaint, ads: the outilstice.com review calls the ads somewhat intrusive — [outilstice](https://outilstice.com/en/?p=2655). Common Sense Education's expert review listed distracting ads as a drawback — [Common Sense Education via search](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/lyricstraining). (Common Sense Education paused edtech reviews in Jan 2026 and planned to remove review pages in Feb 2026, so that page may be gone — [Common Sense Education FAQ page](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/lyricstraining).)
- Complaint, no translations or explanations (free web): it builds listening and memorization but offers no translations or explanations — [All Language Resources](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/?p=18335). The same site, comparing it with Lirica, says LyricsTraining lacks vocabulary and grammar explanations — [All Language Resources, Lirica review](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/lirica/).
- Complaints from a teacher's review (2021): captions don't always keep pace with the song, and educator-written captions aren't always accurate. Gap words in catalogue exercises are random, not targeted. Some videos and lyrics aren't suitable for all audiences. Scores are motivational rather than usable for assessment. Learners can spend too much time choosing songs — [FLTMAG 2021](https://fltmag.com/lyricstraining/).

**Sounter**
- Visible BR reviews are positive: highly recommended (RaySVieira, 02/08/2020); very interactive and delivers on its promise (Jelorobaal, 06/11/2024). One reviewer (Anna_Bloedom, 13/07/2020) loved the format but doubted whether you can actually learn English this way — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/sounter-aprender-ingles/id1505600864).

**Lirica**
- Praise: a clever, fresh, engaging approach; lessons clearly organized by level and song; artists add cultural context — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/lirica-learn-languages). Strong support for comprehension and memorization; the library grows weekly; purchases support artists — [All Language Resources, 2024-05-20](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/lirica/).
- Complaints: activities are the same for every song (repetitive); each lesson downloads separately and fills storage; the free tier is limited; pop songs bring mature or sexual content and the app wasn't built for kids — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/lirica-learn-languages).

**LyricFluent**
- Praise: the concept and the variety of ways to learn — App Store reviewer, via [search summary of App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/learn-languages-w-lyricfluent/id1626804408?uo=4).
- Complaints: when trying a new song, only the intro is playable before a subscription prompt. Search returns nothing even for previously studied songs or artists. Selection is limited beyond the most popular global artists, though users can request songs. One annual subscriber said they would not renew unless the issues were fixed — [App Store listing via search](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/learn-languages-w-lyricfluent/id1626804408?uo=4); [Lingopie roundup via search](https://lingopie.com/blog/apps-to-learn-language-music/).

**Musixmatch**
- Complaints (BR App Store): no YouTube Music or Deezer integration (13/05/2024); sync with music apps broken and lyrics not appearing even on the paid version (23/12/2018); can't set the app language separately from the phone (26/10/2017) — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/musixmatch-letras-de-m%C3%BAsicas/id448278467).
- Translations aren't available for all songs or users — [Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Live-Ideas/Translate-Lyrics-to-other-languages/idc-p/5775878/highlight/true).

**Letras.mus.br**
- Praise: connects to Spotify and pulls lyrics while a song plays (cristinamet96, 24/07/2019). Complaints: wants YouTube Music integration, and background playback not working (Lucas_gsouza, 17/08/2020); the keyboard covers the search field (17/01/2020); playlist edits lost after the first save (JulianaFischer, 27/07/2025) — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/letras-mus-br/id773347891).

**Lingopie**
- Recurring Trustpilot complaints: unexpected charges for lifetime plans or renewals after trials, difficult cancellation, and a ~$40 hold on the card at free-trial sign-up released after ~10 days that wasn't clearly disclosed. One user said they were "scammed into a subscription" (1-star) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lingopie.com?page=6); [Sikayetvar complaint thread](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/lingopie-us/i-cannot-cancel-my-lingopie-subscription-and-keep-getting-billed-what-steps-work-q-36739).

**FluentU**
- Praise: a musician reviewer loved the large selection of music videos — [Test Prep Insight](https://testprepinsight.com/reviews/fluentu-review/).
- Complaints: the UX feels clunky, especially the YouTube embed with transcripts layered on top. Videos are short and disconnected and not for absolute beginners. Quizzes had overlapping audio clips and odd cuts — [Test Prep Insight](https://testprepinsight.com/reviews/fluentu-review/). It is expensive and has no speaking practice — [All Language Resources (2021)](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/author/brian/page/4/). A 2026 review rates it strong for listening and weak for speaking, with minimal grammar explanation — [learnenglish.life 2026](https://learnenglish.life/reviews/fluentu/).

**Language Reactor**
- Limitations: desktop Chrome only (no mobile or TV), and it needs your own Netflix subscription — [Stork](https://www.stork.ai/en/language-reactor). A competitor (Lingopie) argues Pro is hard to justify because speech recognition is inconsistent and Anki export is clunky. Treat as biased — [Lingopie blog](https://lingopie.com/blog/language-reactor-review/).

**Adjacent signal: lyrics behind paywalls**
- In 2024 Spotify restricted lyrics to Premium, and free users complained heavily, including on Reddit. A community idea to restore them was later marked "implemented" (Feb 2025, per search summary) — [Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/?p=301853); [DailyO](https://dailyo.in/news/is-spotify-planning-to-make-you-pay-to-see-song-lyrics-41382); [Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Implemented-Ideas/how-about-NOT-MAKING-US-PAY-TO-SEE-THE-LYRICS/idc-p/6042581/highlight/true).

### Inferences
- The main friction for the dominant BR product (LingoClip/LyricsTraining) is the **3-plays-per-day cap**: users complained about it as early as 2020 and it was still the free limit in the 2026 listing. A free, unlimited model with optional rewarded ads targets exactly this pain. Fill the Song already advertises "unlimited songs per day" free, though.
- Sync accuracy and caption quality are a known weakness of user-contributed lyric catalogues. A curated, verified-sync catalogue could be a quality differentiator.
- "No explanations or translations" in the free gap-fill tier is an opening for Portuguese-language explanations of words, phrasal verbs and slang. That fits a Brazil-only product.
- Mature content in pop songs is flagged by two independent reviewers (Common Sense, FLTMAG). Content filtering or rating matters if teens or schools are a target.
- Users care about getting *their* song (catalogue complaints for LyricsTraining and LyricFluent). A request-a-song flow, as Musixmatch and LyricsTraining already offer, is table stakes.

### Gaps
- **Reddit (r/languagelearning, r/EnglishLearning, r/brasil) could not be accessed**: the fetch tool is blocked from reddit.com and domain-restricted search is refused. No Reddit opinions are included, so this is a material gap for user sentiment.
- No Reclame Aqui complaints were found for Sounter or LingoClip, and the search was inconclusive. Google Play BR reviews (usually the richest BR complaint source) were not retrievable.
- Sounter's visible reviews are all positive; no complaint data on its paywall or price ladder was found.
- No user reviews were found for Fill the Song, Lyrkit or Singit.
- Product Hunt and Trustpilot data for LyricsTraining was not found.

## 3. How products handle lyrics licensing; legal cases about lyrics and YouTube use

### Takeaway
Lyric copyrights belong to songwriters and publishers, not to lyric sites. Products split into those that claim licences (Lirica: "licensing agreements with each label", Sony-backed; LyricFluent: "we legally license lyrics and pay royalties"; Musixmatch and LyricFind as B2B licensors) and YouTube-embed gap-fill sites that state no licence (LyricsTraining's footer says "personal use and educational purposes"; Fill the Song says it is "seeking licensing partners"; LyricsGaps and Lyrkit say nothing). The lyric-licensing market is currently in an antitrust fight (LyricFind v. Musixmatch/TPG, 2025–2026) over Musixmatch's exclusive Warner Chappell deal. YouTube's API policies constrain overlays, audio-only play, caching and charging for embedded content.

### Cited Findings

**Who owns lyric rights; Genius v. Google**
- The court held that Genius does not own the copyrights to the lyrics it transcribes; songwriters and publishers do. Genius sued on contract/terms-of-service grounds, alleging Google scraped its lyrics. The case was dismissed in Aug 2020, the Second Circuit affirmed in March 2022 (contract claim preempted by the Copyright Act), and the Supreme Court denied certiorari in June 2023 — a denial, not a merits ruling. Google said it licenses lyrics from third parties and does not scrape — [Dinsmore](https://www.dinsmore.com/publications/supreme-court-declines-to-resolve-50-million-genius-lawsuit-that-claimed-google-copied-its-website-content/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/us-supreme-court-rejects-google-case-over-stolen-song-lyrics); [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-06-supreme-court-lawsuit-accusing-google.amp).

**NMPA enforcement against unlicensed lyric sites**
- In 2013 the NMPA sent takedown notices to ~50 unlicensed lyric sites (list compiled by researcher David Lowery). Named top offenders were rapgenius.com, lyricsmania.com and lyricstranslate.com. The stated goal was licensing deals, not shutdowns — [Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/682774/data-center-music-industry-seeks-a-refrain-from-unlicensed-lyrics-sites.html); [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-gb/blog/hotforsecurity/music-industry-fights-unlicensed-lyrics-websites).
- In 2014 the NMPA sued SeekLyrics.com and LyricsTime.com. Of the 50 sites contacted, 11 had licensed and 11 had removed content. The NMPA pointed operators to LyricFind, Gracenote and Harry Fox Agency for licences — [NMPA](https://www.nmpa.org/?p=1133). Earlier suits against LiveUniverse and against Motive Force/Sean Colombo were resolved — [NMPA](https://www.nmpa.org/publishers-successfully-resolve-infringement-suit-against-motive-force-and-sean-colombo/).

**LyricFind v. Musixmatch & TPG (antitrust, ongoing)**
- Filed 2025-03-06 in federal court in San Francisco. LyricFind alleges Musixmatch's "first-of-its-kind" exclusive licence with Warner Chappell blocks rivals from offering Warner lyrics. Spotify (Musixmatch's largest customer) ended advanced talks with LyricFind and renewed with Musixmatch. iHeartRadio switched to Musixmatch at a price allegedly more than 5x its previous LyricFind rate. Musixmatch calls the suit meritless — [Billboard, 2025-03-07](https://ca.billboard.com/business/legal/lyricfind-sues-rival-musixmatch-in-antitrust-suit-over-unprecedented-warner-licensing-deal).
- In Sept 2025 Judge Corley dismissed parts of the case but let the core proceed — [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/news/2025-09-06-us-judge-lets-lyricfind-antitrust-case-against-musixmatch-proceed.html.md). In Oct 2025 Musixmatch signed AI licensing deals with Sony Music Publishing, UMPG and Warner Chappell covering more than 15M works. In May 2026 LyricFind filed a second amended complaint of over 80 pages. On 2026-09-02 Musixmatch launched Pro for Labels and LyricFind a free Verified Artist Program. On 2026-09-03 the parties told the court they could not agree on whether to mediate. Musixmatch says more than 250,000 rights owners and publishers trust its database — [AI Musicpreneur, 2026](https://www.aimusicpreneur.com/ai-music-news/lyricfind-musixmatch-antitrust-mediation-collapse-2026/) (a single secondary source, not court filings).
- DSPs rely on LyricFind or Musixmatch to turn plain lyrics into machine-readable, time-synced data — [RightsTech](https://rightstech.substack.com/p/lyricfind-sues-rival-musixmatch-over).

**Lyric licence cost signals (old or unofficial)**
- Musixmatch terms quoted around 2015–16: data licences start at a $25,000 annual minimum, with web/mobile billed per thousand lyric views at no less than $1.40 CPM. This is a decade old; treat it only as an order-of-magnitude reference — [Audacious redmine thread](https://redmine.audacious-media-player.org/issues/502).
- A third-party API directory says Musixmatch's free API returns only a ~30% lyric preview, and that full or synced lyrics and commercial use need paid licensed tiers. This is unconfirmed by Musixmatch — [freeapihub](https://freeapihub.com/apis/musixmatch).

**Licensing statements by learning products**
- Lirica: "licensing agreements with each label" — [All Language Resources](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/lirica/). Sony Music is an investor and helps with licensing — [EdSurge 2019](https://edsurge.com/news/2019-09-23-lirica-raises-1-million-to-improve-app-for-language-learning-through-song).
- LyricFluent: licenses lyrics, pays royalties to artists and uses professional translators, which it gives as the reason for the subscription — [LyricFluent FAQ](https://lyricfluent.com/faq).
- LyricsTraining: footer says "only for personal use and for educational purposes". No licence statement was found on the homepage. Lyrics are partly educator-submitted and reviewed by LyricsTraining — [lyricstraining.com](https://lyricstraining.com/); [FLTMAG](https://fltmag.com/lyricstraining/).
- Fill the Song: seeking music-licensing partners; no licence announced — [fillthesong.com](https://fillthesong.com/en/).
- Letras.mus.br: offers human translations and paid courses. No licensing details were found in the sources reviewed — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/letras-mus-br/id773347891).

**YouTube API Services Developer Policies (relevant to YouTube-embed gap-fill games)** — [YouTube Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)
- I.6: you may not modify, build upon or block any portion or functionality of the YouTube player.
- I.4 and III.F.2.c: you may not remove, obscure or disable player links or YouTube attribution.
- III.F.1: you may not change or interfere with YouTube UIs without prior written approval.
- I.5: you may not modify, block or replace YouTube-served ads.
- I.7 and I.8: you may not separate, isolate or separately promote the audio or video components. I.9: no background-player playback.
- III.E.1.a–b: no downloading, caching or storing of YouTube audiovisual content, and no offline playback, without written approval.
- III.F.3.a: you must not charge users to watch content in an embedded YouTube player.
- III.G.1.c: you may not sell ads, sponsorships or promotions placed on or within YouTube content or the player without approval.
- III.G.1.d: ads on a page or screen containing YouTube API Data are restricted unless other independent content is present.
- The Required Minimum Functionality document (e.g., player size) was referenced but not retrieved.

### Inferences
- A YouTube-embed gap-fill game avoids *recording and video* licensing (YouTube carries the licensed video) but **not lyrics licensing**. Displaying full lyric text with blanks is reproduction of the lyric work, which belongs to publishers. LyricsTraining has operated for 10+ years with user-contributed lyrics and an "educational use" disclaimer, and no lawsuit against it was found. That is survivorship, not legal clearance (inference; needs a Brazilian IP lawyer). Brazil-specific lyric rights (e.g., ECAD collects for performance, not lyric display) were not researched.
- Under the YouTube policies above, gap-fill UI should sit *outside* the player iframe, not as an overlay. There should be no audio-only or background mode, no offline caching, and rewarded ads should not gate the act of watching a YouTube video in the embed (III.F.3.a) or appear on or within the player (III.G.1.c). Gating *game features* (extra hints, lives, duels) behind rewarded ads is likely safer than gating *video plays*. This is an inference to validate with counsel and YouTube's RMF document. A "3 plays per day unless premium" model like LingoClip's may sit uncomfortably with III.F.3.a, but no enforcement against it was found (inference).
- The LyricFind v. Musixmatch dispute shows licensing via a single vendor can get expensive (5x price claims) and concentrated. A small free PWA probably can't afford full licensed lyric text at launch. Options: short excerpts, user-generated lyric lines, publisher deals for a curated set, or partnering with a BR lyrics holder such as Studio Sol/Letras (inference).

### Gaps
- No lawsuit or takedown specifically against a language-learning lyric game (LyricsTraining, Sounter, Lirica, etc.) was found.
- No current (2025–2026) official Musixmatch or LyricFind API pricing was found.
- How Sounter, Lyrkit or Fill the Song source lyrics is not disclosed.
- NMPA 2025–2026 enforcement activity was not found; the cited enforcement is 2013–2014.
- Brazilian law and case law on lyric display (Lei 9.610/98, ECAD vs publishers' graphic and display rights) was not researched.
- YouTube's Required Minimum Functionality (player size, etc.) was not retrieved.

## 4. Gaps users repeatedly say no product fills

### Takeaway
Across products, unmet needs cluster around: (1) generous free access without a hard daily cap or early paywall, (2) the user's own songs, current and local, beyond a small licensed set, (3) accurate sync and quality lyrics, (4) explanation and translation of what you just heard, in the learner's language, (5) real progression and targeted practice instead of random blanks and repetitive activities, (6) speaking and pronunciation, (7) mobile-first access, and (8) honest billing. Social competition already exists (LyricsTraining Friend Challenge, Fill the Song leaderboard), so on its own it is not an unfilled gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Free-tier cap or paywall:** 3 plays/day on LingoClip/LyricsTraining, with a 2020 request for 5 — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/lyricstraining/id1192698323); [Canaltech 2021](https://canaltech.com.br/apps/como-aprender-ingles-com-musica-com-app-lyrics-training/). LyricFluent paywalls after a song's intro — [App Store listing via search](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/learn-languages-w-lyricfluent/id1626804408?uo=4). Lirica's free tier is limited — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/lirica-learn-languages). Musixmatch caps translations unless Premium — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/musixmatch-letras-de-m%C3%BAsicas/id448278467).
- **Catalogue and "my song isn't there":** [App Store BR, LyricsTraining/LingoClip, 2020 review](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/lyricstraining/id1192698323); [British Council comment](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/comment/83845); LyricFluent's catalogue is thin beyond top global artists — [App Store via search](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/learn-languages-w-lyricfluent/id1626804408?uo=4).
- **Sync and caption accuracy:** captions lag, and educator-written captions are inaccurate — [FLTMAG](https://fltmag.com/lyricstraining/). Musixmatch sync broken with music apps — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/musixmatch-letras-de-m%C3%BAsicas/id448278467).
- **No explanation of meaning:** LyricsTraining has no translations or explanations — [All Language Resources](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/?p=18335); [All Language Resources, Lirica](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/lirica/).
- **Pedagogy and progression:** random gap words, scores not useful for assessment — [FLTMAG](https://fltmag.com/lyricstraining/). Repetitive activities — [Common Sense Media, Lirica](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/lirica-learn-languages). FluentU videos are disconnected — [Test Prep Insight](https://testprepinsight.com/reviews/fluentu-review/). A Sounter user doubted the method actually teaches English — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/sounter-aprender-ingles/id1505600864).
- **Speaking:** FluentU has no speaking practice — [All Language Resources](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/author/brian/page/4/); [learnenglish.life 2026](https://learnenglish.life/reviews/fluentu/). Sounter and Cake target this with karaoke pronunciation scoring and speaking practice — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/sounter-aprender-ingles/id1505600864); [Canaltech](https://canaltech.com.br/apps/melhores-aplicativos-para-aprender-ingles-com-musica/).
- **Platform reach and integrations:** Language Reactor is desktop-only — [Stork](https://www.stork.ai/en/language-reactor). Users want YouTube Music or Deezer integrations in Musixmatch and Letras — [App Store BR Musixmatch](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/musixmatch-letras-de-m%C3%BAsicas/id448278467); [App Store BR Letras](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/letras-mus-br/id773347891).
- **Ads and billing trust:** intrusive ads on LyricsTraining — [outilstice](https://outilstice.com/en/?p=2655). Billing and cancellation traps at Lingopie — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lingopie.com?page=6).
- **Content appropriateness:** explicit content in songs and videos — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/lirica-learn-languages); [FLTMAG](https://fltmag.com/lyricstraining/).
- **Discovery overload:** users spend too long choosing songs — [FLTMAG](https://fltmag.com/lyricstraining/). LingoClip added playlist goals and levels in Sept 2024, which suggests it is addressing guided paths — [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/lyricstraining/id1192698323).

### Inferences
- The most defensible positioning for a free BR PWA seems to be the combination of: unlimited free play (rewarded ads unlock extras, not video plays); a curated, sync-verified catalogue of songs Brazilians actually listen to, including current hits, with song requests; Portuguese explanations of the blanked words, chosen by CEFR level or skill (phrasal verbs, connected speech) rather than at random; a guided path or "trilha" that solves song-choice paralysis; and friend duels as engagement rather than the core differentiator. No single product found combines all of these. Fill the Song comes closest in positioning and should be watched closely.
- PWA (no install) plus mobile-first addresses the desktop-only limits of Language Reactor and the install friction of native apps. No user source explicitly requested a PWA, so this is inference.
- Transparent, no-trial-trap monetization (rewarded ads only) is itself a trust differentiator, given Lingopie's billing complaints.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence of Brazilian users explicitly asking for duels or 1v1 with friends, or for rewarded-ad models. Reddit and Google Play BR were inaccessible.
- No quantitative data (survey or review mining at scale) was found ranking which complaint is most frequent. The frequency claims above come from a qualitative read of a few dozen reviews and expert reviews.
- No data on retention or engagement benchmarks for music-based learning apps was found.
